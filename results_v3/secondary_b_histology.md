# v3 secondary analysis (b): histology check of the onset delay (2026-10-28)

Registered in PROTOCOL.md Sect. 10.2 (b). Literature only; no simulation; does not alter the frozen results (results_v3/FREEZE_v2_v3.md). Kept outside PROTOCOL.md so that the frozen protocol hash is unchanged.

Question. v3 (freeze form) assumes no tissue change before tau (estimates 21-24 d from the six cases; 16-17 d from A + B). Is this compatible with observed early tissue formation in sheep osteotomies?

## Evidence

1. Epari DR, Schell H, Bail HJ, Duda GN (2006). Instability prolongs the chondral phase during bone healing in sheep. Bone 38:864-870. 64 sheep, 3 mm mid-shaft tibial osteotomy, rigid or semi-rigid external fixator; 2, 3, 6, 9 weeks.
   - 2 weeks, radiology: "the osteotomy gap is clearly visible in both fixation groups and there were no visible signs of bony callus formation."
   - 2 weeks, histology: "woven bone formation was present on the periosteal surface of the cortical bone fragments in both fixation groups"; "Bone formation was rarely observed endosteally or intercortically"; soft callus of fibrous tissue; haematoma remnants in nearly all semi-rigid specimens.
   - 3 weeks: periosteal callus "increased visibly in size with continued woven bone formation"; laterally "beginning to bridge the gap"; chondral tissue at the ossification fronts.
   - 6 weeks: periosteal bridging laterally in most animals.
2. Vetter A, Epari DR, Seidel R, Schell H, Fratzl P, Duda GN, Weinkamer R (2010). Temporal tissue patterns in bone healing of sheep. J Orthop Res 28:1440-1447. Same 64-sheep series; BA/TA of the bony callus present from the first time point (2 weeks) and increasing linearly; healing stage II reached at about 2.8 weeks (rigid).
3. Claes LE, Heigele CA (1999). J Biomech 32:255-266 (Ulm metatarsal model, the source of the ST13 cases). Fluorochrome labels at 4 and 8 weeks; all animals killed at 9 weeks; no histology before 9 weeks. In vivo IFM of a 3 mm gap, 1.2 mm IFM sheep: 1.16 mm after 1 week. Simon et al. (2011, CMBBE 14:79-93) derived the 0.22 mm/day bone formation rate from these labels (6.2 mm in 28 days, weeks 4-8); the rate before week 4 was not measured.
4. Simon et al. (2011), Discussion: "As long as the stiffness of the newly formed callus was lower than the stiffness of the external fixator kfix, the movement did not change significantly"; animals "were not able to fully bear the weight on their treated limbs in the first 2 weeks".

## Verdict

- The freeze form is not compatible with the histology: in the Berlin sheep tibia model, periosteal woven bone is present at 2 weeks and the callus grows during week 3. Indirect evidence (different bone, fixator and laboratory), but consistent across 64 animals.
- The same sources show that the early tissue is mechanically minor: no radiological bony callus at 2 weeks, no endosteal or intercortical bone, bridging only from about 3 weeks laterally and at about 6 weeks in most animals.
- For the Ulm metatarsal model, no histology before 9 weeks and no bone formation rate before week 4 exist in the sources checked.
- Consequence for Paper 2a: tau must be reported as an effective latency of the IFM response (a delay of the mechanically effective healing), not as biological quiescence. Candidate mechanisms named in the sources, not tested here: early tissue with low stiffness relative to the fixator (Simon et al. 2011), periosteal bone formation remote from the gap before bridging (Epari et al. 2006), reduced limb loading in the first 2 weeks (Simon et al. 2011). A mechanistic form (tissue formation from day 0 with delayed mechanical competence) is a model change and belongs to a later, separately registered study.
